Hotel Calder.

P—01 Hospitality · Adaptive reuse Arts District, Los Angeles · 34.0407° N Completed 2025
01 Overview

A 1920s cold-storage warehouse, reborn as a 142-key hotel — with no visible mechanical.

The design team wanted the board-formed concrete and timber ceilings left untouched. That meant no dropped soffits in guest rooms, no rooftop clutter visible from the pool deck, and NC-25 sound levels at the bed.

Ironline joined at 50% schematic design under a design-assist agreement with the general contractor and carried the mechanical scope through commissioning and into a five-year service contract.

02 Challenge

Exposed structure, landmark facade, zero tolerance for visible equipment.

Floor-to-floor heights of 11′-4″ left 14 inches for all horizontal distribution. The historic facade ruled out through-wall units, and the restaurant’s wood-fired kitchen sat directly below guest floors.

  • No dropped ceilings in 142 guest rooms
  • Rooftop pool deck sightlines to protect
  • Kitchen exhaust riser through six occupied floors
03 Solution

Heat-recovery VRF hidden in casework, fresh air delivered from the corridor.

We selected slim ducted fan coils that fit inside entry closets, with linear slot diffusers set into custom millwork. A dedicated outdoor air system with energy recovery supplies tempered ventilation through the corridor plenum, decoupling fresh air from room comfort.

  • 8 heat-recovery condensers set behind a 6′ acoustic screen
  • Fire-rated grease duct enclosure with ESP pollution control at roof
  • LOD 400 BIM coordinated with structural steel and sprinkler mains
  • 1,840-point BACnet BMS with guest-room occupancy setback
04 Outcome

Quieter than spec, cheaper to run, early to open.

Guest rooms measured NC-22 at commissioning. Simultaneous heating and cooling through heat recovery cut first-year HVAC energy well below the Title 24 baseline, and the mechanical scope closed out three weeks ahead of the GC’s schedule.
