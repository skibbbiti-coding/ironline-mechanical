01 Type I hood exhaust

Grease exhaust built to NFPA 96 and the California Mechanical Code.

We fabricate and install welded grease duct, upblast and utility-set exhaust fans and fire-rated enclosures, coordinated with the hood manufacturer and the fire-suppression contractor.

  • Liquid-tight welded grease duct with cleanouts
  • Rated shaft or listed field-applied wrap
  • Roof clearances to intakes and property lines
02 Make-up air

Replacement air that keeps the kitchen negative and the dining room comfortable.

Tempered make-up air is interlocked with each exhaust fan and balanced so the kitchen stays slightly negative to the dining room. Smoke and cooking odor stay on the line, and the front of house stays comfortable.

03 Permits & approvals

Plan check, inspections and health-department coordination.

Kitchen exhaust work in Los Angeles County generally requires mechanical plan review, fire-department review of the suppression system, building inspections and environmental health sign-off before opening. Requirements vary by jurisdiction, so we confirm the path with the local authority during preconstruction.

04 Pollution & odor control

For wood-fire, charbroil and kitchens under residences.

Electrostatic precipitators, carbon and odor-control modules are specified where exhaust discharges near neighbors or occupied floors, and where air-quality rules for commercial charbroiling apply.

FAQ Commercial Kitchen Ventilation

Questions owners and architects ask.

What is the difference between a Type I and a Type II hood?

Type I hoods capture grease and smoke from appliances such as fryers, grills and ranges, and require welded grease duct and fire suppression. Type II hoods handle heat and steam from dishwashers and ovens that don't produce grease.

Why does a restaurant need make-up air?

Every cubic foot exhausted by the hood must be replaced. Without dedicated make-up air, doors become hard to open, hoods stop capturing smoke and gas appliances can draft poorly.

What approvals are needed for kitchen exhaust in Los Angeles County?

Typically mechanical plan check and permits, fire-department review of the hood suppression system, building inspections and environmental health approval. Exact requirements depend on the city or county jurisdiction, and we coordinate them as part of preconstruction.

How often should kitchen exhaust be serviced?

Inspection and cleaning intervals depend on cooking volume and type, ranging from monthly for high-volume charbroil and wood-fire to semi-annual for moderate volume, per NFPA 96. Fan belts, bearings and make-up air interlocks are covered in our service plans.

Can you add a kitchen to a building that wasn't designed for one?

Often yes. In adaptive reuse projects we route grease duct through new rated shafts or along the exterior, and size make-up air and pollution control so the restaurant works alongside residential or office tenants.
