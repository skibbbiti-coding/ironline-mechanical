01 Dense urban sites

Zero-lot-line buildings and alley-only access.

Most Downtown and Arts District projects have no laydown space. We stage deliveries just-in-time, pre-fabricate duct and pipe spools off site and coordinate deliveries around neighboring businesses, loading docks and alley restrictions.

02 Rooftop crane logistics

Planned weeks ahead, executed in a morning.

Rooftop units, VRF condensers and exhaust fans are set by mobile crane under street-use permits with traffic control. We sequence picks so a single crane day sets every piece of rooftop equipment, then curb, flash and connect while the street reopens.

  • Lift plans and rigging by certified crews
  • Weekend and early-morning picks
  • Structural verification of roof framing
03 Adaptive reuse

Converting cold storage, garment and industrial buildings.

Exposed timber and board-formed concrete are why people want these buildings, and why there is nowhere to hide ductwork. We favor VRF and small-diameter distribution, route mains along structure lines, and work with the architect to make exposed spiral duct an intentional detail.

04 Title 24 & local review

Compliance documented, not improvised.

HVAC alterations trigger California Title 24 energy requirements, and many conversions also go through local building and fire plan review. We prepare mechanical compliance documentation during design-assist and coordinate inspections through turnover.

FAQ Downtown & Arts District

Local questions.

How do you get equipment onto Downtown LA rooftops?

Usually with a mobile crane under a street-use permit, scheduled early on a weekend morning with traffic control. Where crane access is impossible, we use alternatives: knocked-down units, rooftop hoists or interior freight routes.

Do older Arts District buildings need special mechanical planning?

Yes. Unreinforced or retrofitted masonry, timber roofs and limited floor-to-floor heights shape where equipment can sit and how ductwork can run. We verify roof structure and penetrations with the structural engineer before selecting equipment.

How does Title 24 affect a Downtown retrofit?

Altering or replacing HVAC triggers California Title 24 energy requirements, such as equipment efficiency, economizers, controls and duct sealing where applicable. We document compliance as part of design-assist.
